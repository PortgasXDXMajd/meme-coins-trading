## The desk's baseline

The desk trades momentum: it enters a token that is moving and exits within minutes to hours. These are properties of every token it trades. They are accepted risks of the mandate, already reflected in the stop and the exits, so on their own they are neither a reason to reject a specific token nor a rebuttal:

- Tokens are {min_age_minutes:.0f} minutes to {max_age_days:.0f} days old with market caps of ${min_market_cap:,.0f} to ${max_market_cap:,.0f}. Most will eventually go to zero; the desk does not care about eventually.
- The debate decides a clearance, not a moment. A token that wins is cleared for {clearance_minutes:.0f} minutes, and code buys it, with no further debate, the moment it passes the desk's momentum trigger: price up {momentum_min_change:.0f}% to {momentum_max_change:.0f}% in the last 5 minutes (or {fast_change:.0f}% in about {fast_seconds:.0f} seconds and still at its high) inside an hour that is up but no more than {momentum_max_hour:.0f}%, 5-minute volume at least {momentum_surge:g}x its own trailing-hour average, at least {momentum_buy_share:.0f}% of 5-minute trades being buys, and a trailing hour before those 5 minutes in which sellers led (no more than {momentum_max_trailing_share:.0f}% of its trades were buys). Just before the buy, code also cancels it if the price has fallen back below the trigger price or buys no longer lead. The dossier's last fact says whether the token has already triggered or is only warming up.
- So whether it is moving this minute is not the question; code answers that at the buy. The question is whether a breakout entry on this token during the clearance is likely to lose money: who can sell into it, whether the desk can get out, and whether the token is building or fading over the last hours.
- Sellers leading the last hour is an entry condition, not a warning. The desk only buys a breakout that follows a seller-led hour, because on its own record breakouts after an hour of buying were usually the end of the move. So "sells led the last hour" on its own is neither a reason to reject nor a rebuttal; a decline that is still running over 6 hours, with volume drying up, is a different and valid point.
- Pools are thin. The filter requires only ${min_liquidity:,.0f} of liquidity, so pool liquidity is usually a small fraction of market cap. In a constant-product pool whose liquidity is 10% of market cap, a single sale of 1% of supply moves the price by roughly 30%. Large price impact from a hypothetical big sale is normal here.
- Sharp moves in both directions within minutes are normal. Bot-sized trades and many look-alike small wallets are normal on pump.fun-style launches.
- Each position is {max_position_pct:.0f}% of equity. The dossier's round-trip quote shows what the desk itself would pay to get in and out.
- Risk sets the stop at entry from the token's own one-minute volatility, between {stop_loss_pct:.0f}% and {max_stop_loss_pct:.0f}% below entry. At +{take_profit_pct:.0f}% it sells part of the position and moves the stop to at least break-even. From +{trail_activation_pct:.0f}% the stop trails the high.

A point built on a baseline property carries weight only when the data shows this token is materially worse than the baseline right now, or shows a specific holder or group that is positioned and active to trigger it during the trade.
